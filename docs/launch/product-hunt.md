## First comment (the maker post)

```
Hi Product Hunt 👋

I applied to a batch of jobs last spring and typed my own address maybe forty times.
Browser autofill got the email field right and then quit exactly where the actual work
was: the cover letter, the "why this company", the "tell us about yourself".

So Formless does both halves.

It maps form fields to a profile you own — reading how each field describes itself
rather than guessing from position, so it survives React forms, dropdowns, shadow DOM,
the usual mess. And for the long answers, it drafts from your CV using the AI model
already built into Chrome.

The part I actually care about: it makes zero network requests. Not "anonymised
analytics" — zero. There's no backend. Your profile sits in your browser, optionally
AES-GCM encrypted with a key derived from your master password. The AI runs on-device
via Gemini Nano, so no prompt and no CV fragment ever travels. MIT licensed, so you
don't have to take my word for any of it.

Two things it deliberately won't do: submit forms for you, and touch CAPTCHAs. It fills
and drafts; you review and send.

Also in there: import your CV as a PDF or your LinkedIn export to build the profile in a
minute, and a "save what I typed" button that learns from forms you filled by hand —
with passwords, card numbers and OTPs filtered out.

Free forever for the local tier. Later there may be an optional paid tier for
higher-quality drafting on a hosted model and encrypted cross-device sync, but the local
version isn't a trial for it.

What I'd love from you:
• Sites where it mis-fills. Detection improves case by case and bug reports with a URL
  are worth more than stars.
• Whether the on-device model is good enough for your cover letters, honestly.
• Fields you needed and it didn't have.

Happy to answer anything about the crypto, the detection heuristics, or why I didn't do
BYOK API keys.
```

## Answers to have ready

**"How is this different from 1Password / Dashlane / LastPass autofill?"**
They fill credentials and payment details. Formless fills the application — employment history, education, custom questions — and writes the free-text answers. Different job, and it's not a password manager: don't put your passwords in it.

**"How is this different from Simplify / LazyApply / the job-apply bots?"**
Those aim at volume and often auto-submit. Formless never submits and never touches CAPTCHAs, and it doesn't run on a server holding your CV. It's a faster hand, not an autopilot.

**"Google is putting AI in Chrome autofill anyway."**
Probably. The bet is that open source, on-device-only, and CV-grounded drafting is a different product from a browser-vendor feature — and you can audit this one.

**"Is Gemini Nano actually good enough for a cover letter?"**
It writes a solid first draft grounded in your CV and the posting, and the panel makes you edit before inserting. It's not Claude. That's exactly why a hosted paid tier is on the roadmap rather than shipped as the default.

**"Why not BYOK — let me paste my own API key?"**
Key handling turns a zero-network extension into one that holds credentials and phones out, which breaks the main promise for everyone to serve a minority. On-device costs nobody anything and leaks nothing.

**"Is my data really not leaving?"**
Open DevTools → Network and use it. Then read the source. Both take about a minute.

**"Windows / Firefox / Safari?"**
Windows: yes, any Chromium browser. Firefox: build exists, unverified — help wanted. Safari: no, would need a separate Xcode wrapper.

**"What happens if I forget the master password?"**
The data is gone. The key is derived from the password and stored nowhere. Keep a JSON export.

## Metrics worth writing down

Not for a dashboard — just so the next launch is better informed.

- PH: rank at 6h/12h/24h, upvotes, comments, referral clicks
- Store: installs day 1 / day 7, uninstall rate, review sentiment
- GitHub: stars, and more usefully — issues opened, site reports, PRs
- The real signal: **site reports**. Ten of those beat a thousand stars, because they're the thing that makes the product work.
